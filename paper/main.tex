\begin{center}
    Abstract
\end{center}

This study maps how research on large language models (LLMs) is organised
across three streams: LLM-based agents and multi-agent systems, models that run
on a device or in a browser, and applications to inventory management in small
businesses. The data are 489 journal articles published from 2023 to 2026,
retrieved from OpenAlex on 28 October 2026 and screened with language-model
assistance. Performance analysis, bibliographic coupling, co-citation analysis,
and co-word analysis are combined. The co-word analysis yields three clusters,
one centred on agents, retrieval-augmented generation, and business
applications, and two on edge deployment and small language models. The
business-oriented cluster stays separate from the edge clusters in 62 of 63
robustness runs, although between a fifth and a half of the link weight crosses
cluster boundaries. In the co-citation network the few references on generative
AI in supply chains are co-cited with general work on reasoning and agents,
whereas work on efficient inference dominates the foundations of the field. The
co-citation clusters change with small changes in the data and the algorithm
settings and are not stable. The
study offers a first joint science map of these streams, shows that the
evidence on LLMs for inventory management is thin, and outlines a research
agenda.

Keywords: large language model; multi-agent system; on-device inference;
inventory management; bibliometric analysis
