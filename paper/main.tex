\textbf{Mapping the Thematic Structure and Emerging Trends of Large Language Model Research Across Multi-Agent Systems, On-Device Deployment, and Inventory Management: A Bibliometric Analysis}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
Abstract
\end{center}

This study maps how research on large language models (LLMs) is organised across three streams: LLM-based agents and multi-agent systems, models that run on a device or in a browser, and applications to inventory management in small businesses. The data are 485 journal articles published from 2023 to 2026, retrieved from OpenAlex on 28 October 2026 and screened with language-model assistance. Performance analysis, bibliographic coupling, co-citation analysis, and co-word analysis are combined. The co-word analysis yields three clusters, one centred on agents, retrieval-augmented generation, and business applications, and two on edge deployment and small language models. The business-oriented cluster stays separate from the edge clusters in all 63 robustness runs, although between a fifth and a half of the link weight crosses cluster boundaries. In the co-citation network the business stream rests on only a few references on generative AI in supply chains, whereas work on efficient inference dominates the foundations of the field. The larger co-citation clusters change with the algorithm settings and are not stable.
