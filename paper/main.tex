\textbf{Mapping the Thematic Structure and Emerging Trends of Large Language Model Research Across Multi-Agent Systems, On-Device Deployment, and Inventory Management: A Bibliometric Analysis}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
Abstract
\end{center}

The purpose of this study is to map how research on large language models (LLMs) is structured across three streams, namely LLM-based agents and multi-agent systems, models that run on the device or in the browser, and applications in inventory management for small businesses, through a bibliometric analysis. The study analyses 485 journal articles published between 2023 and 2026, retrieved from OpenAlex on 28 October 2026 and screened with language-model assistance. It combines performance analysis, bibliographic coupling, co-citation analysis, and co-word analysis. Co-word analysis yields three clusters: one around agents, retrieval-augmented generation, and business applications, and two around edge deployment and small language models. The business-oriented cluster is loosely separated from the edge clusters in all 63 robustness runs, but about a fifth to a half of the link weight crosses clusters. In the co-citation network, the business-related stream is a very small group of references on generative AI in supply chains, while efficient-inference work dominates the foundations of the field; the large co-citation clusters are not stable across algorithm settings. The study provides a first science map across these streams, shows that evidence on LLMs for inventory management is thin, and sets out a research agenda. \todo{check the claims against the final screened dataset and the manual validation}
