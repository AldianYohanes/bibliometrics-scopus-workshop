\section{Result and Analysis}

The initial search returned 820 records, which formed 801 unique records. After removing duplicate titles, excluding Zenodo items, and screening on title and abstract, 485 journal articles published between 2023 and 2026 remained (Table~\ref{tab:flow}). Together they have 2{,}905 citations (global OpenAlex counts), an average of 5.99 citations per article, and an h-index of 24 (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}). Output grows quickly: 4 articles in 2023, 37 in 2024, 151 in 2025, and 293 in 2026, where 2026 is a partial year. Citation counts follow the age of the articles (2 citations for the 2023 articles, 844 for 2024, 1{,}902 for 2025, and 157 for 2026), so the low figure for 2026 reflects time, not weak influence.

The overall co-citation partition is not stable. Removing two further suspect references changed the result from four clusters (133, 124, 85, and 4 references) to the five clusters above, and in 21 repeated runs (20 seeds and a greedy algorithm) the number of clusters was between three and six. The network is also very dense (about 14{,}000 links among 344 references), because articles with long reference lists create many pairs. We therefore do not rely on the exact composition of the large clusters. One feature is more robust: four references on generative AI and AI in supply chains (Jackson 2024, Wamba 2023, AlMahri 2025, and Kosasih 2023) had at least three of the four in the same small cluster of four to eleven references in 17 of the 21 runs; in the other four runs (three seeds and the greedy algorithm) they were absorbed into a large mixed cluster of 70 to 156 references. The data therefore support a modest statement: references on generative AI in supply chains are co-cited mainly with each other and rarely with the efficient-inference literature, so the business stream has no broad shared foundation yet, but it is a very small group.

Only 22 of the 183 connected articles come from the agent or inventory blocks. We repeated the Louvain clustering with 20 random seeds and a greedy algorithm. The number of clusters varied from 11 to 15, and between 50\% and 73\% of these 22 articles (median 73\%) fell in clusters in which business-related articles were the majority. The business stream is therefore a small and partly stable feature of the coupling network, not a robust cluster with fixed membership.

\subsection{Thematic Map and Emerging Topics}
We placed each co-word cluster on a centrality--density map (Figure~\ref{fig:tema}, Table~\ref{tab:L}). With the eight-occurrence threshold, the compression cluster has high density and high centrality (a well-developed, central theme), the small-model and privacy cluster has high centrality and lower density (a transversal, basic theme), and the agent and business cluster has the lowest centrality (213) and a middle density: it is the least connected to the rest of the field. At a threshold of five occurrences the business-oriented cluster again has the lowest centrality. With only three clusters this is a coarse picture, and the quadrant labels depend on the cut-off.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the Search Strings}
We re-ran the searches with five variations (Table~\ref{tab:J}). All variations that only widen the strings retained all 485 articles of the final dataset; the title-only variant retained just 61 (13\%), so searching abstracts is necessary. Narrow changes (dropping the stock-market exclusion, adding inventory synonyms such as SKU or stockout) changed the result by fewer than ten records. Widening the language-model terms (generative AI, GPT, ChatGPT, foundation models) or the device terms (TinyML, local LLM, smartphone, mobile device) added 512 and 576 records outside the cleaned baseline; a part of them are the 93 baseline records removed as duplicates or Zenodo items. We read eight added titles for each of the four widened variants: most were marginal (for example generic generative AI in commerce, or smartphone applications), but a few were relevant, such as work on offline document analysis with local language models. So the core of the dataset is stable, the boundary of the field is wide, and our baseline favours precision over recall. The added records were not screened, so we cannot say how the maps would change. \todo{screen the records added by V1 and V4 and repeat the co-word analysis}
