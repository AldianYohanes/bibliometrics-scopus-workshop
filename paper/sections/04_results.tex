\section{Result and Analysis}

The initial search returned 820 records, which formed 801 unique records. After duplicate titles and Zenodo items were removed and the remainder screened on title and abstract, 485 journal articles published between 2023 and 2026 were left (Table~\ref{tab:flow}). Together they have 2{,}905 citations by OpenAlex's global counts, an average of 5.99 citations per article, and an h-index of 24 (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}). Output rises steeply, from 4 articles in 2023 to 37 in 2024, 151 in 2025, and 293 in 2026, a year that is not yet complete. Citations follow the age of the articles: the 2023 articles have 2 citations in the dataset, those of 2024 have 844, those of 2025 have 1{,}902, and those of 2026 have 157. The low figure for 2026 therefore reflects the time available for citation, not weak influence.

The overall partition is not stable. Removing two further suspect references changed the result from four clusters (133, 124, 85, and 4 references) to the five reported here, and across 21 repeated runs (20 seeds and a greedy algorithm) the number of clusters ranged from three to six. The network is also very dense, with about 14{,}000 links among 344 references, because an article with a long reference list creates many pairs at once. The exact composition of the large clusters should therefore not be relied on. One feature is more robust. Four references on generative AI and AI in supply chains (Jackson 2024, Wamba 2023, AlMahri 2025, and Kosasih 2023) had at least three of the four in the same small cluster of four to eleven references in 17 of the 21 runs, and in the other four runs (three seeds and the greedy algorithm) they were absorbed into a large mixed cluster of 70 to 156 references. The data thus support a modest conclusion: references on generative AI in supply chains are co-cited mainly with each other and rarely with the efficient-inference literature. The business stream has no broad shared foundation yet, but it is a very small group.

\subsection{Bibliographic Coupling}
Of the 485 articles, 278 have reference data, and 183 of these share at least two references with another article. Without a citation threshold they form 13 clusters of 43, 38, 35, 24, 14, 8, 5, 5, 3, 2, 2, 2, and 2 articles, of which seven have fewer than six articles. To obtain three to five clusters of at least six articles, as the supervisor's rule requires, only articles cited at least three times were kept. The threshold has a cost for the business stream. Of the 22 agent and inventory articles among the 183 connected ones, only 3 remain, and the cluster on LLM agents for supply chain and demand forecasting (five articles in the unfiltered map) is no longer visible. \subsection{Thematic Map and Emerging Topics}
Each co-word cluster was also placed on a centrality--density map (Figure~\ref{fig:tema}, Table~\ref{tab:L}). At the eight-occurrence threshold, the compression cluster has both high density and high centrality, which marks a well-developed central theme. The small-model and privacy cluster has high centrality but lower density, as a transversal basic theme would. The agent and business cluster has the lowest centrality (213) and a middling density, so it is the least connected to the rest of the field. At a threshold of five occurrences the business-oriented cluster again has the lowest centrality. With only three clusters the picture is coarse, and the quadrant labels depend on the cut-off.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the Search Strings}
The searches were repeated with five variations (Table~\ref{tab:J}). Every variation that only widened the strings retained all 485 articles of the final dataset. The title-only variant retained 61 (13\%), so searching abstracts is necessary. Narrow changes, such as dropping the stock-market exclusion or adding inventory synonyms like SKU and stockout, altered the result by fewer than ten records. Widening the language-model terms (generative AI, GPT, ChatGPT, foundation models) or the device terms (TinyML, local LLM, smartphone, mobile device) added 512 and 576 records outside the cleaned baseline; part of each figure consists of the 93 baseline records removed as duplicates or Zenodo items. Eight added titles were read for each of the four widened variants. Most were marginal, for example generic generative AI in commerce or smartphone applications, but a few were relevant, such as work on offline document analysis with local language models. The core of the dataset is therefore stable, the boundary of the field is wide, and the baseline favours precision over recall.
