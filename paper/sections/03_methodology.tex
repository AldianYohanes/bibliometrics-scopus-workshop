\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Bibliometric approach}
Bibliometric analysis studies bibliographic data quantitatively, through performance analysis and science mapping \parencite{donthu2021,zupic2015}. This study uses four techniques that answer different questions. Citation analysis identifies the most influential documents, co-citation analysis reveals the foundations of the field, bibliographic coupling shows its current research streams, and co-word analysis maps its concepts.

\subsection{Search string}
Records were retrieved from OpenAlex through its public interface on 28 October 2026. \todo{cite the OpenAlex paper after verification: arXiv 2205.01833 could not be verified} The search covered titles and abstracts, was restricted to the document type ``article'', and covered the publication years 2023 to 2026, of which 2026 is incomplete. OpenAlex was chosen because it is open and its queries can be repeated. Most bibliometric guidance assumes Scopus or Web of Science \parencite{donthu2021}, and the choice between these databases is discussed by \textcite{zhu2020}, \textcite{mongeon2016}, and \textcite{echchakoui2020}. The consequences of using OpenAlex instead are discussed in the Discussion section.

Table~\ref{tab:search} lists the search strings. Each row holds synonyms joined with OR, and the rows are combined into three blocks, one for each stream. Block A (LLM agents in business settings) combines rows 1, 3, and 4 with AND. Block B (LLMs in the browser or on the device) combines rows 2 and 5. Block C (LLMs for inventory) combines rows 2, 6, and 7 and excludes records that match row 8.

\subsection{Screening and final dataset}
The three blocks returned 149, 584, and 87 records, 820 in total, which formed 801 unique records (Table~\ref{tab:flow}). Sixteen records shared a normalised title with another record; in each case the version with a journal source, reference data, and a DOI was kept. A further 77 records hosted on Zenodo were excluded, because Zenodo is a repository without a guaranteed peer-review process. The remaining 708 records were screened on title and abstract.

Screening was assisted by language models in two passes. In the first pass, eight Claude Haiku instances judged about 89 records each as relevant, not relevant, or uncertain. A record counted as relevant when a language model and at least one of the three topics were a main or important part of the work. The pass produced 430 relevant, 150 not relevant, and 128 uncertain records. In the second pass, three Claude Sonnet instances reassessed the 128 uncertain records using longer abstracts and reported a confidence level. They resolved 55 records as relevant and 72 as not relevant and left one uncertain. The final dataset contains 485 records. \todo{human validation: report the agreement on the 20-record sample; 97 of the 128 second-pass decisions had low model confidence}

\subsection{Analysis}
Performance analysis counted publications per year, source, institution, country (from author affiliations), and author, together with citations. Authors, sources, and institutions were counted by OpenAlex identifiers rather than names. Science mapping used three techniques \parencite{donthu2021,zupic2015}.

Co-word analysis used the keywords that OpenAlex assigns to each record. Synonymous forms were merged (for example ``LLM'' and ``large language models''), and ``large language model'' and ``artificial intelligence'' were dropped because they occur in nearly every record. Keywords with at least eight occurrences were kept, which gave 68 keywords.

Bibliographic coupling and co-citation rely on reference lists. OpenAlex held reference data for 255 of the 485 records. Crossref data, matched through DOIs, raised the total to 278 records (57.3\%). Two records were treated as linked when they shared at least two references. For co-citation, references cited by at least three records in the dataset were kept. Ten items were removed as data artefacts. Some were proceedings volumes, which are not single works. Three were references whose OpenAlex titles do not fit the articles that cite them: one is cited by 22 edge-LLM articles although its title concerns intelligent tutoring systems, and two are cited by 9 and 7 edge-LLM articles although their titles concern AI consciousness and behavioural safety. These look like linking errors in OpenAlex, but this was not verified.

The thresholds of three citations and eight occurrences were chosen so that each map contains a readable number of items (344 references and 68 keywords). Figure~\ref{fig:ambang} shows how many items remain at other thresholds. The curves fall steadily and have no sharp break, so the choice is a judgement, and the robustness checks below vary the co-word threshold.

Clusters were found with the Louvain algorithm \parencite{blondel2008}, computed in Python (networkx, fixed random seed) and displayed in VOSviewer 1.6.21 \parencite{vaneck2010} through its map and network file formats. The cluster membership shown in the figures is therefore the one computed in Python. Robustness was tested in several ways. The co-word clustering was repeated with minimum occurrences of 5, 8, and 12, with 20 random seeds, and with a greedy modularity algorithm. The coupling and co-citation clustering were repeated with 20 seeds and the greedy algorithm. Five variations of the search strings were also run. Cluster labels are proposals made by reading the abstracts of each cluster's representative articles (the reference titles, for co-citation) with language-model assistance, and the author has yet to confirm them. The target of 200 to 1{,}000 records follows the sample-size guidance attributed to \textcite{rogers2020} \todo{read the paper to confirm the range}. The record flow follows the PRISMA statement \parencite{page2021}.
