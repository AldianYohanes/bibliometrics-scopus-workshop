\section{Discussion}

Three patterns stand out. First, the field is dominated by work on running language models at the edge: the most cited articles, the largest coupling clusters, the foundations in the co-citation network, and two of the three co-word clusters all concern efficient inference, compression, and privacy-preserving training. Second, the business-oriented stream (agents, retrieval, and inventory or supply chain applications) is smaller, less connected to the rest, and not stable in its exact composition. It appears in the co-word map as a cluster with the lowest centrality, in the coupling network as two small groups, and in the co-citation network as a very small group of references. Third, there are early signs of a shift: between 2025 and 2026 the share of articles with agent, retrieval, and small-business keywords rises while the share with generic edge keywords falls.

The three streams share concerns, such as cost, latency, privacy, and reliability, but they build on different literatures. The inventory articles that we found cite supply chain and operations management work and are not linked to the efficient-inference literature through shared references or citations.
