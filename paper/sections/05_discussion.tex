\section*{Discussion}

Three patterns stand out. First, the field is dominated by work on running language models at the edge: the most cited articles, the largest coupling clusters, the foundations in the co-citation network, and two of the three co-word clusters all concern efficient inference, compression, and privacy-preserving training. Second, the business-oriented stream (agents, retrieval, and inventory or supply chain applications) is smaller, less connected to the rest, and not stable in its exact composition. It appears in the co-word map as a cluster with the lowest centrality, in the coupling network as two small groups, and in the co-citation network as a very small group of references. Third, there are early signs of a shift: between 2025 and 2026 the share of articles with agent, retrieval, and small-business keywords rises while the share with generic edge keywords falls.

The three streams share concerns, such as cost, latency, privacy, and reliability, but they build on different literatures. The inventory articles that we found cite supply chain and operations management work, for example the generative AI studies of \textcite{jackson2024} and \textcite{wamba2023}, and are not linked to the efficient-inference literature through shared references or citations. Because screening and the search strings favour precision (Table~\ref{tab:J}), we do not claim that no bridging work exists; we claim that, in this dataset, it is rare.

The direction of the early trend agrees with an earlier bibliometric review of agentic AI in small, medium, and micro enterprises, which reports a sharp rise since 2022 driven by LLMs and multi-agent systems \parencite{olujimi2025}. The two studies differ in data (OpenAlex journal articles from 2023 to 2026 here, against Scopus, Google Scholar, and grey literature from 2019 to 2025 there) and in scope, so we read the agreement as consistent, not as confirmation. The broad bibliometric review of the LLM literature ended in early 2023 \parencite{fan2024}; our data start in 2023 and, within the three streams that we selected, are dominated by edge deployment. This dominance has two possible readings: the edge literature may simply be larger, or our block B terms (on-device, edge device) may be more common in titles and abstracts than the terms of blocks A and C. The sensitivity check does not settle this, so the size differences between streams should not be read as a ranking of research importance. \todo{discuss why the streams diverge; this needs the author's interpretation}

\subsubsection*{Limitations}
The study has the following limitations, each linked to a direction for future work.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One database.} We used OpenAlex, not Scopus or Web of Science. Coverage and metadata quality differ \todo{cite OpenAlex coverage studies after reading them, e.g.\ the reference coverage analysis by Culbert et al.\ (2025), found by search but not yet read}. Future work should repeat the search in Scopus or Web of Science and compare the maps.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete reference data.} Only 278 of 485 articles (57.3\%) have reference lists, which limits coupling and co-citation. Many missing lists come from journals that do not deposit references.
  \item \textbf{Partial year.} 2026 is incomplete, so yearly counts and trends for 2026 must not be read as a full year.
  \item \textbf{Search sensitivity and breadth.} Search is limited to titles and abstracts. Widening the language-model or device terms adds about five hundred records each; they were not screened, so the baseline favours precision and the effect on the maps is unknown.
  \item \textbf{Model-assisted screening.} Screening used language models; \todo{report the manual validation and its agreement}. Low-confidence decisions remain.
  \item \textbf{Keywords.} Keywords come from OpenAlex's automatic assignment, not from the authors.
  \item \textbf{Unequal blocks.} The three search blocks differ in how common their terms are, so the relative size of the streams partly reflects the search design (see the Discussion).
  \item \textbf{Cluster stability and labels.} Co-word membership was stable but modularity was low. The co-citation partition changed when two references were removed and varied between three and six clusters across runs; only the small supply chain group was consistent. Cluster labels are model-assisted proposals from reading representative articles and are not yet confirmed.
  \item \textbf{Metadata errors.} Three references had titles that do not fit their citing articles and were removed; other OpenAlex metadata errors may remain. A part of the author, institution, and country data is missing (country data for 24\% of articles).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Conclusion}
This study mapped 485 journal articles from 2023 to 2026 across three streams of LLM research: agents in business settings, models on the device or in the browser, and inventory management. Four findings follow from the analyses. First, work on edge deployment dominates the dataset: all ten most cited articles, the five largest coupling clusters, and the main co-cited foundations concern efficient inference, although this is partly a consequence of how the search blocks are defined. Second, the business-oriented stream is small and loosely connected: its co-word cluster has the lowest centrality, it forms two small coupling clusters, and its co-citation foundation is a group of a few references on generative AI in supply chains. Third, an early shift is visible between 2025 and 2026, with a growing share of articles on agents, retrieval, and small enterprises. Fourth, the checks show that the separation of the co-word clusters is stable but loose, while the co-citation partition is not stable and should not be interpreted in detail.

The study contributes a first joint science map of these streams, to our knowledge and within the limits of an OpenAlex-only search; a reproducible pipeline with stability and sensitivity checks; and a research agenda. For practitioners, the evidence on LLMs for inventory management in small businesses is thin and recent, so it should be tested on their own data. The agenda is to repeat the search in Scopus and Web of Science, to screen the records added by wider search strings, to read the supply chain and inventory articles in full, and to build and evaluate agent-based inventory systems that run on local or in-browser models. \todo{the author should confirm the contribution claims after the Scopus or Web of Science recheck}
