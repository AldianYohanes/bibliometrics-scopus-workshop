\section*{Discussion}

The analyses point to three findings. First, work on running language models at the edge dominates the dataset. The most cited articles, the largest coupling clusters, the foundations of the co-citation network, and two of the three co-word clusters all concern efficient inference, compression, and privacy-preserving training. Second, the business-oriented stream, which covers agents, retrieval, and inventory or supply chain applications, is smaller, less connected to the rest of the field, and unstable in its exact composition. In the co-word map its cluster has the lowest centrality, in the coupling network only three business articles pass the citation threshold (without it they form two small groups), and in the co-citation network it is a very small group of references. Third, a shift is beginning. Between 2025 and 2026, the share of articles with agent, retrieval, and small-business keywords rises, while the share with generic edge keywords falls.

The three streams share concerns such as cost, latency, privacy, and reliability, yet they build on different literatures. The inventory articles found here cite supply chain and operations management work, for example the generative AI studies of \textcite{jackson2024} and \textcite{wamba2023}, and are not linked to the efficient-inference literature through shared references or co-citation. Because the screening and the search strings favour precision (Table~\ref{tab:J}), the claim is not that bridging work does not exist, but that in this dataset it is rare.

The direction of the early shift agrees with an earlier bibliometric review of agentic AI in small, medium, and micro enterprises, which reports a sharp rise since 2022 driven by LLMs and multi-agent systems \parencite{olujimi2025}. The two studies differ in data, since this one uses OpenAlex journal articles from 2023 to 2026 and the earlier one used Scopus, Google Scholar, and grey literature from 2019 to 2025, and they also differ in scope. The agreement is therefore consistent with the earlier finding but does not confirm it. A broad bibliometric review of the LLM literature ended in early 2023 \parencite{fan2024}; the present data start in 2023 and, within the three selected streams, are dominated by edge deployment. That dominance has two possible explanations. The edge literature may simply be larger, or the terms of block B (on-device, edge device) may be more common in titles and abstracts than those of blocks A and C. The sensitivity check does not settle the question, so differences in size between the streams should not be read as a ranking of research importance. \todo{discuss why the streams diverge; this requires the author's interpretation}

\subsubsection*{Limitations}
The study has several limitations, and each points to a direction for further work.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One database.} The data come from OpenAlex, not Scopus or Web of Science, and coverage and metadata quality differ \todo{cite OpenAlex coverage studies after reading them, for example the reference coverage analysis by Culbert et al.\ (2025), found by search but not yet read}. The search should be repeated in Scopus or Web of Science and the maps compared.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete reference data.} Only 278 of the 485 articles (57.3\%) have reference lists, which limits coupling and co-citation. Many of the missing lists come from journals that do not deposit references.
  \item \textbf{Partial year.} The year 2026 is incomplete, so yearly counts and trends for 2026 do not describe a full year.
  \item \textbf{Search sensitivity and breadth.} The search covers titles and abstracts only. Widening the language-model or device terms adds about five hundred records each, and these were not screened, so the baseline favours precision and the effect on the maps is unknown.
  \item \textbf{Model-assisted screening.} Screening relied on language models. In a check of 20 low-confidence decisions the author agreed with the model on 8 and rated the other 12, all excluded by the model, as relevant. The dataset may therefore omit relevant records, and the check does not estimate overall accuracy. \todo{decide whether to repeat the analysis with the second-pass exclusions added}
  \item \textbf{Keywords.} Keywords come from the automatic assignment in OpenAlex, not from the authors.
  \item \textbf{Unequal blocks.} The three search blocks differ in how common their terms are, so the relative size of the streams partly reflects the search design.
  \item \textbf{Cluster stability and labels.} Co-word membership was stable but modularity was low. The co-citation partition changed when two references were removed and ranged from three to six clusters across runs, and only the small supply chain group was consistent. Cluster labels are model-assisted proposals based on representative articles and are not yet confirmed.
  \item \textbf{Metadata errors.} Three references whose titles do not fit their citing articles were removed, and other OpenAlex errors may remain. Author, institution, and country data are partly missing; country data are absent for 24\% of the articles.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Theoretical implications}
The maps suggest that research on LLMs for business operations has not yet drawn on the large body of work on efficient and local inference. The field is better described as three loosely connected streams than as one. \section*{Conclusion}
This study mapped 485 journal articles from 2023 to 2026 across three streams of LLM research: agents in business settings, models on the device or in the browser, and inventory management. Four findings emerge. Work on edge deployment dominates, since all ten most cited articles, all four coupling clusters, and the main co-cited foundations concern efficient inference, although the definition of the search blocks partly drives this result. The business-oriented stream is small and loosely connected, with the lowest centrality among the co-word clusters, only three business articles in the thresholded coupling map, and a co-citation foundation of only a few references on generative AI in supply chains. An early shift is visible between 2025 and 2026 towards agents, retrieval, and small enterprises. Finally, the separation of the co-word clusters is stable but loose, while the co-citation partition is unstable and should not be interpreted in detail.

The study contributes a first joint science map of these streams, to the authors' knowledge and within the limits of an OpenAlex-only search, together with a reproducible pipeline that includes stability and sensitivity checks, and a research agenda. For practitioners, the evidence on LLMs for inventory management in small businesses is thin and recent and should be tested on their own data. The agenda has four parts: repeating the search in Scopus and Web of Science, screening the records added by wider search strings, reading the supply chain and inventory articles in full, and building and evaluating agent-based inventory systems that run on local or in-browser models. \todo{the author should confirm the contribution claims after the Scopus or Web of Science recheck}
